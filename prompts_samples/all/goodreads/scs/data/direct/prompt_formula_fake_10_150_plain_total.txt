** spoiler alert ** 
 The Goldfinch starts with an un-named man hiding out in a hotel room in Amsterdam, scanning papers about a murder. Before you can even start to wonder what is going on you are taken back 14 years to the life of Theo Decker, 13 years old and suspended from school for smoking. Early for a visit with his teacher, he and his mom stop off at Metropolitan Museum; his mom loves art and there is a piece she really wants to see - The Goldfinch of the title. Once there, they are separated. It is then the bomb goes off. In the confusion that follows, Theo makes it out - with the painting. His mom doesn't. Nothing really seems to go right for him after that. 
 First he finds himself being put up by friends, the flown to Vegas by his absentee father, where he is completely ignored by everyone but his only friend Boris, before ending back up in New York at the door of an art-restorer who also lost someone in the bombing. Through all of this, the painting stays with; something that makes him afraid because it is stolen, but something he can't let go of because it connects him to his mother. It is his anchor as his life unravels until a chance (or not so chance) meeting with his childhood friend Boris. 
 The Goldfinch is a hard book to describe because it covers so many topics. More than anything, it is a book about loss and grief. Theo is a lost soul that I felt for terribly. But it is not a sad book. There are some funny moments, some scary moments, so exciting moments as the books comes full circle to the opening chapter and just who is in the hotel room in Amsterdam. It is also a book that builds slowly. Somewhere along the line, I found I was staying up later and later to finish it.

Audrey is enamoured with a painting by Fabricitus called The Goldfinch. Theo's not really listening. He is too enamoured himself with a red haired girl and her hunchback grandfather.

Finally, I hit the ground on the same road I started on. 
 I wonder to myself: What the hell was point of that? 
 What the hell was the point of The Goldfinch? Because I am still trying to figure out.

He was debating to kill him himself after the 70 or so pages that were Theo's and Boris' Russian drug mob action movie sequence. 
 I wholeheartedly believe that Tartt cannot write likeable characters to save her life. It still sounded pretentious. It wasn't as bad as The Secret History but was pretty glaring. I assume she takes a decade to write each book because Tartt wants to put every painstakingly minute detail she's ever learned in those 10 years in her novel. The end result is dense drivel akin to someone's brain turning to mush but they keep on talking and talking. 
 It is still amazing to me how much The Goldfinch deteriorated for me. It was like when the most popular kid promises to be your friend then beats you up and steals your lunch money.

 Identify at least 2 characteristics of this genre and subgenre and discuss how they appear in your book: 
 This novel is a piece of historic YA fiction because accurately shows a time in the south in the United States when we were beginning to enforce desegregation laws.     3. For each unique 'book_id', isolate all its associated reviews.
    4. Calculate the 'Base_SCS' by summing the SPS values of all reviews belonging to that 'book_id'.
    5. Filter the reviews of that 'book_id' to isolate only those with a positive SPS.
    6. For this filtered subset of positive reviews, calculate the 'Validation_Weight' by taking the 'n_votes' of each positive review, adding 1 to it, and summing these values together. (If a book has no positive reviews, the Validation_Weight is 0).
    7. Multiply the 'Base_SCS' by the 'Validation_Weight'. This final number is the SCS for that 'book_id'.
    8. Repeat steps 4 through 7 for every unique 'book_id'.
    9. Sort the books based on their final SCS in descending order.
    10.To resolve ties, order books by their id in descending order.
